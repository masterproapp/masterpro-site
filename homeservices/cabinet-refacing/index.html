What we do

If the cabinet boxes are solid, the customer doesn't need a full tear-out. Refacing replaces the doors, drawer fronts, and hardware and covers the frames in new veneer. They get a new look without new countertops or plumbing work.

  • New doors and drawer fronts
  • Veneer on face frames and exposed sides
  • Hinges, pulls, and knobs
  • Soft-close hinge and drawer upgrades
  • Matching trim and filler pieces

How we do it

Every refacing job follows the same process.

  1. Measure and remove

    We measure every opening and take off the old doors, fronts, and hardware.

  2. Cover the frames

    We apply new veneer to the face frames and exposed sides.

  3. Hang the new look

    We install new doors, drawer fronts, hinges, and pulls.

  4. Adjust and clean up

    We align every door and drawer so gaps are even, then clean up.

Questions customers ask

Short answers you can give on the spot.

Should they reface or replace?

If the layout works and the boxes are in good shape, refacing is the lighter project. If they want to move things around or the boxes are damaged, send them to cabinet installation.

Do the countertops stay?

Yes. Refacing works around the existing countertops, sink, and appliances.

Can they use the kitchen during the job?

Mostly, yes. The cabinet boxes stay in place, so the kitchen stays usable, though some doors will be off during the work.

Can they reface bathroom vanities too?

Yes. Add the vanity to the referral and we'll quote it with the kitchen.
