05 / Talking Points

What customers ask at the register.

Quick answers to the questions that come up most. Use these to close the install attach without slowing down the checkout.

Q"Why do I need a measurement visit?"
For built-in appliances (cooktops, wall ovens, built-in microwaves, dishwashers), the cabinet opening and utility connections need to be verified before the install day. The measurement visit prevents wasted trips, surprise charges, and damaged cabinets. It's a one-time $120 charge and it's required.
Q"How fast will they come out?"
MasterPro reaches out within 1 business day of the order being placed. Most installs are scheduled within the first available delivery window — usually 3–7 days depending on appliance availability and the customer's preferred slot.
Q"Can they take the old one away?"
Yes. Add the haul-away SKU at checkout — $50 for a standard appliance, $65 for pro/oversized. The tech removes and disposes of it at the time of install. No separate trip needed.
Q"Are they licensed?"
MasterPro is a licensed contractor. Every tech is background-checked and insured. Customer's home is covered under MasterPro's general liability and the Home Depot service guarantee.
Q"What if they're not happy?"
The install is backed by the Home Depot service guarantee. If anything is wrong, MasterPro will return at no additional cost to make it right. Customers can call MasterPro directly or escalate through the store.
Q"What about parts I might need? (cords, hoses, etc.)"
Many installs require additional parts — dryer cords, water lines, gas connectors, dishwasher hammer arrestors. Sell the parts at the register. If the customer skips them, the tech may have to reschedule, which delays the install and frustrates the customer.
Q"Built-in microwave — do I need a trim kit?"
Yes — always sell the trim kit with a built-in microwave. The trim kit is the frame that fits the microwave into the cabinet opening — without it, the install can't be completed. Trim kits are model-specific, so make sure the customer is buying the one that matches the exact microwave model. The Built-In Microwave w/ Trim install (SKU 13899 · $179) covers the labor.
Q"Do you install built-in refrigerators?"
Yes. Built-in refrigerators are part of our complex install service. They require a pre-install measurement — cabinet opening, water line, electrical, and clearances all need to be verified before delivery. Once measured, we handle the install end-to-end.
Q"Can you install a gas dryer in the garage?"
Yes. We install gas dryers in garages where there's an existing gas line and proper venting. If the gas hose needs to be added or extended, that's a Gas Line add-on ($71) at the time of install.
Q"What if the cabinets or countertops need to be cut?"
We make the cuts. Cabinets, countertops, and stone — all handled by our team on the install day. Cuts are priced per the add-on list (1 cut on cabinets $212 · 2 cuts $267 · 1 cut on stone $247 · etc.). Quote it up front so the customer isn't surprised.
Q"Can you relocate an electrical outlet?"
Yes. We can install a new outlet ($233), relocate a junction box ($234), or do power conversions like 220V to 110V ($314). Custom electrical work like a 50A conversion is quoted after a site assessment.
Q"What about a stacking washer/dryer?"
Yes — and don't forget the stacking kit. The kit is what physically secures the dryer on top of the washer. It's brand- and model-specific, so make sure the customer buys the right one with the units. Without it, the tech can't safely complete the stack.
Q"Dishwasher install — what else can you handle?"
A lot. We can replace angle stops, install or replace toilets and p-traps, and drill a hole for an airgap on the countertop ($212). Add the line items at the register so the tech arrives with everything quoted and ready.
Q"Dishwasher install — what else can you handle?"

Q"Does the dishwasher need an air gap or GFCI?"
No. In Oregon, a dishwasher does not require an air gap or a GFCI, and it can be hardwired. No countertop air-gap hole is needed. (Note: in California these are required — confirm the region is set correctly above.)
Q"Does the dishwasher need an air gap or GFCI?"
Yes. In California a dishwasher requires an air gap and a GFCI. If a countertop air-gap hole is needed, that's a drilled hole add-on ($212) — quote it at the register.
